Help — Running Framer Buddy

Running Framer Buddy

It works the way your hands already expect: type a number, tell it what the number is — feet, inches, or a fraction — and it keeps everything in feet-inch-fraction for you.

Enter a measurement

For 5 feet, 3 and a half inches: tap 5 Feet 3 Inch 1 ⁄ 2. Add, subtract, multiply and divide measurements just like plain numbers — answers come back in feet, inches and fractions. Multiply one measurement by another and the answer is an area instead: it reads in square feet, with square inches and square yards on the line underneath — and dividing that area by a length gives the other length back. Tap ⌫ to back up one step, +/− to flip the sign, and On/C once to clear the entry, twice to clear the whole calculation.

Fraction resolution & display

Tap Fraction res above the keys to cycle how fine fractions read out: 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/64. Tap Disp- Ft/In/Frac to cycle the answer display between feet-inch-fraction, decimal feet and decimal inches.

Roof & rafter triangle

Enter the rise and tap Rise, enter the run and tap Run. Then tap Diag for the rafter length and Pitch for the pitch — tap Pitch again to see it as an angle in degrees. To set a pitch instead, type the pitch (inches of rise per 12 of run) and tap Pitch with a Rise or Run already stored.

Rafters — common, hip & jack

Set the triangle first: enter the run and tap Run, then set the pitch — type the pitch (like 6) and tap Pitch — or enter a Rise instead. Keep tapping Rafter for the common rafter line length, then the plumb cut angle and the level (seat) cut angle. Tap Hip to cycle hip length, hip run and hip pitch (equal-pitch roofs, hip run = run × 1.41421). Set rafter spacing with Spacing — tap it to cycle 12″, 16″, 19.2″ and 24″, or type your own spacing and tap it — then tap Jack for the common difference between successive jacks and the first, second and third jack lengths.

Area & circles

Multiply a length × a width — say 24 Feet × 8 Feet — then tap Area (or =) for square feet; the sub-line also shows square inches and square yards. For a circle, enter the diameter and keep tapping Circ to cycle diameter, circumference and circle area.

Percent, square root & square — and the Off key

% finishes a calculation the way you mean it: type the first number, the operator, the percent, then % — 200 × 10 % gives 20, 200 + 10 % gives 220, 200 − 10 % gives 180, and 200 ÷ 10 % gives 2,000. With no calculation running, % just divides the number on screen by 100 (50 % gives 0.5, shown as 1/2″).
√ gives the square root of the number on screen — 144 √ reads 12.
x² squares the number on screen — 5 x² reads 25. Square a measurement and it works in inches: 2′ x² reads 576 — that’s square inches, 4 ft².
Off blanks the display and puts the keys to sleep; tap On/C to wake it back up. Memory, your kept walls and areas, and the roof figures all stay put while it sleeps.

Studs — wall stud count

Step 1: enter the wall length, for example 21′ 8″ — tap 2 1 Feet 8 Inch.
Step 2: tap Studs once — you get the stud count at 16″ centers. That example wall reads 18 studs.
Step 3: tap Studs a second time — the same wall at 24″ centers. That example reads 12 studs.
Keep tapping to flip back and forth between 16″ and 24″ — the sub-line always names the spacing and the wall length it used. Entering a new wall length starts again at 16″. The count puts a stud at each end of the wall, and the spacing never runs over: leftover part-space at the end still gets its stud. No length typed? Studs will work off the measurement already on screen. Keeping walls: after a count, tap Store to keep that wall in the STORED list — keep every wall on the job, each with its length and spacing, and the list shows the total studs at the bottom.
Running total: with a count on screen, tap + and that count starts a running total. Enter the next wall length and tap Studs — the new count is added in and the display shows the whole total with its breakdown: a 32′ wall gives 25 studs, tap +, a 24′ wall gives 19 more, and the display reads 44 studs (25 + 19). Keep going the same way, wall after wall. At any time, tap Store and the full total is saved in the STORED section — every wall in the run lands there as its own line, and the total at the bottom is the full count. Tapping On/C to clear the screen does not lose the running total.

Sheets — sheathing count

Step 1: work out the area to cover — multiply the length × the width, for example 21′ 8″ × 32′ 3″, which works out to 698.75 ft². Or skip the multiplying and just type the square feet as a plain number (698.75, say) instead.
Step 2: tap Sheets once — you get the count in 4×8 sheets (32 ft² each). That example reads 22 sheets.
Step 3: keep tapping to cycle the sheet size — a second tap gives 4×10 sheets (40 ft² each), 18 on that example; a third tap gives 4×12 (48 ft² each), 15; a fourth tap gives 4×16 (64 ft² each), 11 — and the next tap comes back around to 4×8.
The count always rounds up: a part sheet counts as a whole sheet, and no waste is added on top. A new area starts again at 4×8. The count on the display names its sheet size right with it — 22 sheets · 4×8 — and the sub-line names the area it used and the size the next tap brings up. Keeping areas: after a count, tap Store to keep that area in the STORED list — keep every area, each with its sheet size, and the list shows the total sheets at the bottom.
Running total: Sheets adds up the same way Studs does. With a count on screen, tap + to start a running total, then work the next area and tap Sheets — its count joins the total on the display, breakdown and all. Keep going area after area, and tap Store at any time to save the full total in the STORED section, every area kept as its own line. Working the next area can be as plain as multiplying it out — a length × a width — and tapping Sheets instead of =: Sheets finishes the multiplication and counts from the product. Tapping On/C to clear the screen does not lose a running total — it stays alive until you store it or start a fresh count without +.

Memory & what's stored

Store saves the number on screen to memory, M+ adds to it, and Rcl brings it back. That includes a count straight off the Studs or Sheets key — run the count, tap Store, and it is kept as its own entry. Keep every wall and every area that way and STORED lists them all, with the final total studs and total sheets added up. A running total built with + stores the same way — tap Store and the full total is what gets kept. Tap STORED at the top any time to see everything held on the job — Rise, Run, Diagonal, Pitch, Hip rafter, Jack step, Spacing, Studs, Sheets and Memory. In that window, Clear rafters wipes the roof values (Rise, Run, Diagonal, Pitch, Hip and Jack) and resets spacing to 16″, ready for the next roof. The kept Studs list and the kept Sheets list each carry their own Clear list button in that same window — one tap empties just that list and its total, and leaves everything else (the other list, the roof figures, Memory) untouched. At the bottom of that page, Send by email opens your email app with the whole stored page filled in — just add who it’s going to and hit Send. Export PDF and Export spreadsheet send the whole stored page out as a file instead — exporting opens your share sheet, so you can email the file or send it to another app; where sharing isn’t offered, the file downloads instead.

Browser — Suppliers & Lookup

Tap Browser and this window is a door to websites — nothing else. It opens real sites, never a search about a site.
Project Buddy Professional Services Homepage sits at the very top and opens the Project Buddy website home page. It is locked in place — it cannot be removed — and it stays tappable like every other link.
Google sits under it and opens Google itself — your fallback for finding anything.
Home Depot sits right under Google and the button says only that. It works off the postal code or ZIP at the top of the window — enter the code for the town you want, where the job is, say. For a Canadian postal code it opens Home Depot Canada’s store finder with that town’s stores listed: tap your store there once and Home Depot’s site remembers it — the app itself can’t set your store, only their site can. For a US ZIP it opens the US store finder page, where you search the ZIP there. With nothing entered, it opens the Canadian home page. Enter it once (or tap Use my location) and it’s remembered on this device. It never opens a Google search about Home Depot.
Your own suppliers: tap the yellow Add Your Supplier Url button. Find your supplier on Google first, copy their website address right from there, and paste it in — no typing long addresses from memory. Then name the button yourself, the same look as the Home Depot button, and tap Add supplier. Your yard, your truss plant, whoever you actually buy from — the list is saved on this device and each button opens that supplier’s own website.
Taking one off: every supplier you add carries a small Clear button beside it — one tap and it’s off the shelf. The Project Buddy homepage button, Google and Home Depot are fixed and stay put.
These links open live pages, so they need data; the calculator itself never does.

Convert — the yellow key

Tap Conv, then yds, m, cm or mm to convert the measurement on screen. With Conv lit, Feet gives decimal feet, Inch gives decimal inches, and ⁄ flips between fraction and decimal display.

On a PC keyboard

The number keys, + − * and Enter (for =) all work, / starts a fraction, Backspace deletes, and Esc clears — or closes this Help when it's open.
