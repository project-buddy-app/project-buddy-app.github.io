Help — Running Lift Buddy

Running Lift Buddy

The front screen is a sling-tension calculator and a plain calculator. The trade calculators — rigging, boom geometry, weight from size, outrigger pads and centre of gravity — each get their own screen off the labelled keys.

Rigging — the front screen

Type the load weight in lb, tap Legs to set how many sling legs (it cycles 1, 2, 3, 4), tap Hitch to set the hitch (Vert, Chok, Bask), then tap a sling angle — 90° 60° 45° 30°. The display reads the tension in each leg. The angle is measured from horizontal, and it is the whole ballgame: a 10,000 lb load on 2 legs is 5,000 lb a leg straight up, 5,774 at 60° — and a full 10,000 lb a leg at 30°. Tap Store to keep the result in STORED.

Rig — the full rigging screen

Same math with the fine control: any angle you type, not just the presets, and a sling rating needed per leg line that accounts for the hitch — a choker hitch carries about 3/4 of a vertical leg, so the sling has to be rated higher to do the same work; a basket carries about double. Under 45° the screen warns you the tension is climbing; under 30° it tells you to re-rig — never lift with slings under 30 degrees.

Boom — geometry

Two directions. Length + angle gives the working radius and the tip height. Length + radius gives the angle and the tip height — the "can I reach it, and at what angle" check. Tip height is measured above the boom foot (the boom pin), not above the ground — add the pin height on your machine if you need height over the ground.

Weight — from size

Measure the piece, pick box/slab or cylinder, pick the material. Densities are the standard averages — precast and reinforced concrete at 150 lb a cubic foot, plain concrete 145, steel 490, timber 35 — or type a custom density. A 10 ft × 4 ft × 6 in precast panel reads 3,000 lb. Real pieces vary with rebar and mix, so when there is a weight ticket, the ticket wins.

Pad — outrigger size

Give it the load on one outrigger and the ground you're on, and it sizes a square pad, rounded up to the whole inch. No load figure handy? Give it the gross weight and tap the ¾ button — the worst-corner rule of thumb, because one corner can see about three-quarters of the whole weight on a hard pick. The ground numbers are rough guides — soil varies from one side of a site to the other, so when in doubt, go bigger.

CG — two-point pick

Total weight, the distance between the two pick points, and how far the centre of gravity sits from the left pick. It splits the weight between the ends: 10,000 lb on a 20 ft span with the CG 5 ft from the left puts 7,500 lb on the left end and 2,500 on the right. The end nearer the centre of gravity carries more — rig each end for what it actually carries.

Plain math, memory & the yellow key

The number pad does plain decimal math with ÷ × − + =. Mem holds the number on screen, Rcl brings it back. The yellow Conv key converts the number on screen — keep tapping it to cycle: lb ↔ kg, short tons ↔ lb, ft ↔ m and inches ↔ mm. The line under the display always names the conversion it just did.
