Help — Running Mechanics Buddy

Running Mechanics Buddy

The front screen is a torque converter and a plain shop calculator. The trade calculators — bolt torque, displacement, gears, speed, tires, tap drills and electrics — each get their own screen off the labelled keys.

Torque convert — the front screen

Type the torque, then tap the unit it is in: ft-lb in-lb Nm or kg-m. The display shows your number in that unit and the line underneath reads it in the other three. Tap a different unit to re-read the same value from that unit. Tap Store to keep the spec in STORED — handy for a whole torque sequence on one job.

Plain math, memory & the yellow key

The number pad does plain decimal math with ÷ × − + = — no feet and inches, just numbers, the way a shop calc should. Mem holds the number on screen, Rcl brings it back. The yellow Conv key converts the number on screen — keep tapping it to cycle: hp ↔ kW, psi ↔ bar, psi ↔ kPa, °F ↔ °C and inches ↔ mm. The line under the display always names the conversion it just did.

Bolt — torque from clamp load

Enter the bolt diameter (inches or mm), the clamp load you want (lb or kN), and the K factor for the thread condition — Dry 0.20, Oiled 0.15, Plated 0.16, or type your own. It works out the wrench torque in ft-lb, Nm and in-lb from T = K × D × F. Remember the same bolt takes less torque oiled than dry for the same clamp — that is the K factor doing its job. Always check the manufacturer's spec for the job; this is the math behind the spec, not a replacement for it.

Displ — engine size

Bore, stroke and cylinder count, measured in inches or mm. Gives the displacement in cubic inches, cc and litres. A 4.00 bore, 3.48 stroke, 8 cylinders reads about 350 cu in · 5.7 L.

Gear & Speed

Gear: ring gear teeth ÷ pinion teeth — 41 over 11 reads 3.73. Speed: engine RPM, the transmission gear you are in, the final drive ratio and the tire's overall diameter in inches — it gives road speed in mph and km/h. In top gear at 1.00 with a 3.73 axle on a 28-inch tire, 3,000 rpm works out to about 67 mph.

Tire — sizes & speedo error

Type the sidewall sizes the way they are written on the tire, like 265/70R17. It works out each tire's overall diameter, the change as a percent, and the true speed when the speedometer reads 60 mph and 100 km/h. Bigger tires make the speedo read low — this tells you by how much.

Tap — tap drill chart

Pick the thread — common metric coarse and imperial sizes — and it shows the tap drill three ways: the drill's own name where it has one (like a #7 or letter drill), millimetres, decimal inches, and the nearest 1/64 fraction for the fractional set in the drawer.

Ohm — electrics

Fill in any two of volts, amps, ohms, watts and tap Work it out. A 12-volt circuit through a 4-ohm load draws 3 amps and uses 36 watts — the kind of check that tells you whether a draw is normal or a fault.
