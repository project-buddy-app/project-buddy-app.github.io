Quote Check — a second opinion before you sign

For homeowners. Got a contractor’s quote for a renovation and a pen hovering over it? Before you sign, send it here. It gets taken apart line by line — every price checked against fair market by a carpenter with 45 years in the trade, on your side of the table.

  • Line-by-line breakdown: each price set against a fair range, with the working shown
  • The hooks, named: soft allowances, “to be decided” lines, and extras and change-order terms that only surface at payment time
  • What’s missing from the quote — work that isn’t in it has a way of becoming tomorrow’s extra
  • A straight verdict — sign it, question it, or walk away — plus the questions to put to the contractor in writing first
  • Written report only: no phone call, no sales pitch. It’s a document review, not legal advice

$99 per report (Canadian), flat — whether the verdict is “sign it, it’s fair” or “don’t sign it as written.”

How it works

Plain steps, no runaround

  1. Send your plans. Upload a blueprint set — foundation, floor plan, elevations and details, whatever you have.
  2. Questions first. Anything the plans don’t answer gets asked before a single number is counted — never guessed. Anything missing, conflicting or unreadable goes on an assumptions list for you to confirm.
  3. Get your takeoff. A complete material list in a builder’s format — quantity, description, location, SKU, unit price and total — checked against the building code where your project is, with any plan discrepancies marked right on a copy of the drawings.
  4. Order from it. Prices come from live local suppliers at the time of the job, so the list you order from is the list that was priced.
