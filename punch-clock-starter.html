Settings

Pay rules — overtime

Daily overtime rule onHours past the daily limit pay overtime, on top of the weekly rule

Default is the common setup: overtime at 1.5× past 40 hours in a week. Ontario shop? Set the weekly figure to 44. Hours are shared out in the order you worked them, so a shift that crosses the line splits into regular and overtime parts — the Log and the exports show the split. Every rule here is a setting, never a lock: change one and every report re-figures itself.

Target — the ahead / behind line

Set a daily target — say 8 — and the clock face tells you all day whether you're ahead of it or behind it, and by how much. Leave it at 0 and the line stays off the face.

Rounding

Rounding applies to the Check In / Check Out button, at the moment you tap it. Shifts you type into the Log keep the exact times you give them.

Breaks — automatic deduction

Take a standing break off long shiftsSo lunch never has to be typed in

With this on, a shift longer than the limit gets the standing break taken off automatically — unless you typed break minutes for that shift yourself, which always wins. It's a setting, not a lock: turn it off and the hours come back, everywhere.

Backup & restore — your whole record in one file

The backup file holds everything: every shift, your jobs and tasks, rates, day notes, absence days and every setting on this sheet. Save it somewhere safe — your Drive, your email, a computer. On a new phone, restore from that file and the clock comes back exactly as it was. Nothing to fix, nothing to re-enter.

Checking in and out

Pick the job and task on the face, check the rate, and tap Check In. Your timer starts and the button turns to Check Out — one tap ends the shift and files it in the Log. Add an unpaid break in minutes before checking out and it comes off the shift. The note box rides along with the shift you're finishing. The face shows today and only today — your punches, your total, and whether you're ahead of or behind your target.

Switching mid-shift

Move from framing to cleanup at 2 PM? Change the job or task on the face and tap Switch job / task. Your current shift closes at that moment and a new one opens on the new job — no check-out, no lost minutes, and your day still adds up to one clean run of shifts.

Breaks — typed, or standing

Two ways, your choice. Type break minutes on the face before you check out and they come off that shift. Or turn on the standing break in Settings — a set number of minutes that comes off any shift longer than a limit you pick, so lunch never has to be typed. A break you type always wins over the standing one. Neither is forced: both are settings, and turning the standing break off puts the hours back everywhere.

Day notes & sick, holiday and vacation days

The day note box on the face takes a line about today — the weather, the delivery that never came, why the day ran short. Under Log → Day notes & absence days you can write a note on any day, and mark a day Holiday, Sick or Vacation so an empty day is never a mystery at payroll time. Give the absence a number of hours if it carries any (say 8 for a paid holiday), or leave it at 0. Absence hours are shown in the overviews and on the timesheet for the record — the clock doesn't pay them and they don't count toward overtime; what they're worth is your payroll's call.

Fixing mistakes & past shifts

Tap Log for every shift, newest first, grouped by day with the day's hours and pay. Edit any shift — date, in and out times, break, job, task, rate, note — or Delete it. Forgot to check in yesterday? Add a past shift with the real times, right from the button at the top of the Log. Every report and export re-figures itself from the corrected record. A shift that runs past midnight counts on the day it started; an end time earlier than the start is read as next-day.

How overtime is figured

Your hours are shared out in the order you worked them. Past your weekly limit (40 hrs to start with — 44 for Ontario) they pay the overtime rate, 1.5× unless you change it under Settings. Turn on the daily rule there too if your deal pays overtime past 8 in a day. Check it yourself: one job, Garage build at $35. Five days, in at 7:00, out at 3:30, half an hour unpaid break — 8 hours a day, 40 for the week, $1,400.00 and no overtime. Now add a Saturday shift on a second job, Deck repair at $30, 9:00 to 11:00. Those 2 hours were worked past 40, so they pay 1.5 × $30 = $45 an hour: $90.00, and the week becomes 42 hours and $1,490.00. Those are the numbers this clock shows.

Reports & exports

Reports shows Day, Week and Month views — step through periods with Prev / Next — with hours and pay by job and by day, absence days and day notes included. Before you send anything, choose what's in it: the period on screen, and all your jobs or just one. Then one timesheet goes out three ways, all carrying the same figures: Send by email opens your email app with the timesheet written into the message, ready to address and send. Export PDF and Export spreadsheet make real files — they open your share sheet where this device offers one, so pick Mail there and the file rides on the email as an attachment; where there's no share sheet, the file downloads instead. Export calendar file writes this period's shifts as calendar events you can import into Google Calendar — a second copy of the record, in a second place.

Backup & restore — the phone can break, the record can't

Under Settings → Backup & restore, one tap makes a backup file holding everything: every shift, your jobs, tasks and rates, your day notes and absence days, and every setting. Save it somewhere that isn't this phone — Drive, email, a computer. On a new phone, open the Starter, tap Restore, pick that file, and check the counts it shows you before you confirm. The clock comes back exactly as it was — nothing to fix, nothing to re-enter. A file that isn't a Starter backup is refused and changes nothing.

The face at a glance

The big timer counts your running shift, with the job, task and check-in time under it. The Today panel lists today's punches and your total so far. The strip at the bottom always includes right now: today's hours, this week's hours, overtime this week, estimated pay this week, this month's hours — and, if you've set a daily target in Settings, the ahead / behind line: green when you're ahead of target, red when you're behind. The small BRK flag lights when an unpaid break is entered on the face.
